Fall 2026 Ada County Market: What Home Sellers Should Watch

By Jeff Jones, 208 Premier Real Estate · October 28, 2026

Boise Regional REALTORS® released its September market report on October 9. For sellers preparing a Boise or Meridian home for market, the useful question is how the county figures compare with the homes a buyer could choose instead of yours.

The September snapshot

The figures below cover Ada County single-family homes, including resale and new construction. BRR’s source is Intermountain MLS data as of October 8, 2026.

Median sale price$575,000Down 3.4%

Resale homes had a $561,065 median sale price; new construction had a $622,704 median. Countywide sales were 8.5% above September 2025 despite the monthly slowdown.

Source: BRR September 2026 Ada County Market Stat Sheet. These are county-level figures, not a valuation of an individual property.

Price against the buyer’s actual alternatives

Start with recent comparable sales and the active listings competing for the same buyer. Compare location, condition, layout, lot utility, size, and property type. A county median cannot account for a busy road, unusual floor plan, premium renovation, or the condition of neighboring properties.

Price per square foot is one comparison tool, but it is not a complete valuation method. A smaller renovated home can command a different rate than a larger home needing work. Ask which differences explain the gap before multiplying one nearby sale’s price per square foot by your home’s size.

Use showing feedback as evidence

Set a review date when the listing launches. Track online exposure, showing requests, feedback, and offers separately. Few showings can point to pricing, presentation, access, or a small buyer pool. Repeated comments about one property characteristic deserve attention even when that characteristic cannot be changed.

Improve what you can: accurate photos, clear room descriptions, easy showing access, and a compelling first impression. When the objection is permanent, review whether the price accounts for it. More advertising alone may not resolve a mismatch between the asking price and the alternatives buyers see.

Compare offers by estimated net and certainty

A higher purchase price can come with larger seller concessions or other costs. Put offers side by side using an estimated seller net sheet, then consider financing, contingencies, inspection terms, closing timing, and the buyer’s ability to perform. Review those terms with your broker before deciding which offer is strongest.

For example, an additional $10,000 in price does not create $10,000 in extra proceeds if the offer also asks for $10,000 in additional credits. The relevant comparison is the full package, not the headline number.

Make a property-specific plan for fall

Choose a showing schedule you can maintain, prepare the home for shorter daylight hours, and agree on what evidence would trigger a pricing review. Decide whether to stay listed based on your moving needs, competing inventory, and current buyer response. A seasonal generalization is not enough to decide for every seller.
